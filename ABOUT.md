# Clearance — What it's for and how to use it

This document explains Clearance from the user's point of view: the problem it
addresses, the way of thinking it encourages, and exactly how you work with it
month to month. It assumes no familiarity with budgeting jargon.

---

## 1. The core idea in one sentence

**Clearance is a monthly ritual for looking at the money you actually earned,
deciding on purpose where it goes, confirming that the month "cleared," and then
locking that month away as a permanent record you never have to touch again.**

It is not a transaction tracker, not a forecasting engine, and not a bank
dashboard. It is the place you sit down once a month and *make decisions*.

---

## 2. The problem it's built to solve

Most money apps answer the question *"where did my money go?"* — after the fact,
by importing transactions and showing charts. That's useful for awareness, but it
keeps you in a reactive posture: you observe what already happened.

Clearance answers a different, forward-leaning question:

> **"I have this month's income in hand. What am I going to *do* with it — and did
> I end the month ahead?"**

This matters most for people who:

- have **income that varies month to month** (freelancers, contractors, anyone
  paid by days worked or by project), so a fixed annual budget doesn't fit;
- want to **route surplus into several named savings/investment buckets** on
  purpose, rather than letting it pool in a checking account and evaporate;
- value a **deliberate monthly close** — a moment of intention — over passive
  tracking;
- want a **clean historical record** of each month's decisions that stays exactly
  as they left it.

The name says it: each month you perform a **clearance** — you clear the decks,
route what you earned, and confirm you're in the black.

---

## 3. The mental model

Clearance is built on a small, consistent model. Understanding these five ideas
is enough to use the whole app.

### a. The month is the unit
Everything is organized by month. Each month is its own self-contained review.
You don't edit "a budget"; you fill in *this month*, then move on. Past months
are history — see §7.

### b. Income → obligations → buffer
Within a month, money flows in one direction:

```
Effective income  −  rent  −  actual card spend   =   Remaining Buffer
```

- **Effective income** is what you really earned this month.
- **Rent** and **card spend** are what's already committed/gone.
- The **Remaining Buffer** is the surplus left over — the money you actually get
  to decide about. If it's positive, you *cleared*; if negative, you overspent
  and the app says so plainly.

### c. Spending vs. routing are different acts
This is the key distinction in Clearance:

- **Spend categories** (the "Clearance Check") are money that *left* — rent, card
  spend. They shrink the buffer. You record them to find out what's left.
- **Routing funds** (the "Wealth Engine") are money you *deliberately move*
  toward a goal — an investment portfolio, an emergency fund, a car fund. This is
  the productive use of the surplus. You don't just observe it; you *commit* to a
  target and then *confirm* you transferred it.

Spending is subtraction you record. Routing is allocation you choose.

### d. Target vs. actual / planned vs. confirmed
Throughout, Clearance separates **intent** from **reality**:

- A spend category has a **target** (what you expected) and an **actual** (what
  happened).
- A routing fund has a **target** (what you intend to move) and a **transferred**
  checkbox (whether you've actually done it).

This lets the app show you "planned routing" vs. "confirmed routing," and how
much is **still pending** — i.e. decisions you've made but not yet executed.

### e. The template vs. the month
Your funds and spend categories rarely change month to month, so Clearance keeps
a **template** (in Settings) describing your default set. Every new month is born
pre-filled from that template. But each month is then **yours to adjust** — you
can add a one-off fund, remove one you didn't use, or rename something, *for that
month only*. Editing the template changes *future* months; it never rewrites the
past.

## 4. A month, start to finish

Here's the actual workflow — what you do when you sit down to close a month.

### Step 1 — Start the month
From the sidebar, **Start New Month** (the current month) or **Add Specific
Month** (a past month you're catching up on). The new month arrives pre-filled
with your template categories and your baseline income/rent, so you're editing,
not starting from a blank page.

### Step 2 — Enter income
Two modes, because income comes in two shapes:

- **Fixed:** type the amount you were paid.
- **From days worked:** toggle "Calculate from days worked," then enter how many
  days you worked and your income-per-day. Clearance multiplies them. This is for
  variable-income months — a short month or a heavy month is captured honestly,
  measured against your baseline working days.

### Step 3 — Record what's already committed
Enter **rent**, then fill in the **actual** amount for each **card-spend
category** (each has a target to compare against). These are the real outflows
for the month.

### Step 4 — Read the buffer
The **Remaining Buffer** updates live: income − rent − actual spend. Green with an
up-arrow means **surplus** ("you cleared"); red with a warning means **deficit**.
This single number is the heart of the check: *did this month come out ahead?*

### Step 5 — Route the surplus
In the **Wealth Engine**, each fund shows its target. As you actually make each
transfer in your bank, **check it off**. The **Month Routing** summary tracks:

- **Planned routing** — the sum of all fund targets (your intent).
- **Confirmed transfers** — what you've actually moved.
- **Still pending** — the gap, so nothing falls through the cracks.
- **Transfer progress** — e.g. "3 of 4 complete."

### Step 6 — Sanity-check growth
The **Growth Estimator** takes each fund's target and its own annual
growth-rate assumption and projects a **12-month value** — a rough, motivating
sense of what routing at this level compounds into over a year.

### Step 7 — Look at the trend
**Insights & Analytics** zooms out across months:

- **Buffer Trend** — a chart of your surplus/deficit over recent months.
- **Rolling Spend Average** — your typical card spend lately.
- **Wealth Velocity** — the total you've actually routed into funds over time
  (the cumulative proof the habit is working).

### Step 8 — Close it and leave it
That's the month. You don't "submit" anything — once it's filled in, it stands as
the record. Next month you start fresh, and this month becomes a line in the
sidebar you can revisit but won't disturb.

## 5. Customizing it to your life

Your categories are entirely yours.

- **Rename** anything inline (the example "Car fund" is just a default — make it
  "House deposit," "Wedding," "Taxes," whatever).
- **Add** a spend category or a fund with the "＋ Add" button — per month, on the
  spot (a one-time medical bill this month; a new fund you're starting).
- **Remove** any row you don't need this month (with a confirmation, so it's
  deliberate).
- **Set per-fund growth rates** — a stock portfolio and a savings fund grow at
  different rates, so each carries its own assumption.
- **Manage the template in Settings** — the master list every new month inherits.
  Change it when your financial life changes; past months stay as they were.

The guiding rule: **the template shapes the future; the past is immutable.**

---

## 6. Reading the numbers (a glossary)

- **Effective income** — what you actually earned this month (entered, or days ×
  rate).
- **Remaining buffer** — income − rent − actual card spend. Positive = surplus
  (cleared); negative = deficit.
- **Planned wealth routing** — sum of all fund targets this month.
- **Confirmed transfers** — sum of funds you've checked off as transferred.
- **Pending** — planned minus confirmed; routing you've intended but not executed.
- **12-month projection** — each fund's target compounded for a year at its own
  rate, summed.
- **Rolling spend average** — average actual card spend over the last few months.
- **Wealth velocity** — cumulative confirmed routing across all your months.

---

## 7. Why the history is sacred

Clearance treats every past month as **a closed ledger entry, not a live
balance.** The tagline — *"Route income with intent, then keep the historical
record untouched"* — is a design principle, not decoration:

- Editing your category **template never reaches backward** into months you've
  already done.
- Your decisions for May are exactly what you see when you open May next year.
- The result is a trustworthy diary of financial intention over time — you can
  watch your buffer trend and wealth velocity grow knowing the data was never
  retroactively "tidied."

## 8. A worked example

Say it's the end of the month and you earned **₪10,980** (you worked 22 days at
₪499/day). Your rent is **₪1,300**. You spent **₪2,485** on your main card and
nothing on the secondary one.

```
Effective income          ₪10,980
− Rent                     ₪1,300
− Card spend (actual)      ₪2,485
─────────────────────────────────
Remaining Buffer         ₪7,195   ✅ Surplus — you cleared
```

Now you route that surplus. Your funds this month:

```
Investment portfolio   ₪1,506   (8.0%/yr)   ☑ transferred
Emergency fund         ₪1,000   (3.75%/yr)  ☑ transferred
Car fund                 ₪400   (3.75%/yr)  ☐ pending
Hobby fund               ₪300   (3.75%/yr)  ☐ pending
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Planned routing        ₪3,206       Confirmed ₪2,506   Pending ₪700
Transfer progress: 2 of 4 complete
```

The **Growth Estimator** tells you that routing at these targets projects to
roughly **₪21,000** of contributions-plus-growth over the next twelve months. The
**Buffer Trend** shows this month's ₪7,195 sitting alongside previous months, and
**Wealth Velocity** ticks up by the ₪2,506 you confirmed.

You leave ₪700 of routing "pending" because you haven't made those two transfers
yet — so next time you open the app, it's still asking you to finish the job.

---

## 9. What Clearance deliberately is *not*

Setting expectations is part of the purpose:

- **Not a bank integration / transaction importer.** You enter the figures
  yourself, on purpose. That friction *is* the ritual.
- **Not a long-range financial planner.** The growth estimate is a simple,
  honest one-year projection, not a retirement model.
- **Not a shared or cloud product.** Everything lives locally on your Mac (no
  account, no sync, no server). Your money data is yours.
- **Not a spending-police app.** It won't nag about categories or set limits. It
  hands you the numbers and lets you decide.

In short: Clearance is the quiet, private, once-a-month desk where you turn a pile
of income into a set of intentional decisions — and build a clean record of having
done so, month after month.
